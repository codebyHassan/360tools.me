Financial Management Guide

How to Calculate, Interpret, and Maximize Return on Investment (ROI)

Understand the mathematical formulas behind simple ROI, compound annualized returns (CAGR), and risk-adjusted capital budgeting.

The Core Return on Investment Formula

Return on Investment (ROI) is the universal metric used by executives, venture capitalists, and private investors to assess the efficiency of an allocated dollar. The standard mathematical formula is expressed as:

$$\text{ROI} = \left( \frac{\text{Gross Revenue} - \text{Cost of Investment}}{\text{Cost of Investment}} \right) \times 100$$

Simple ROI vs Annualized ROI (CAGR)

Simple ROI ignores the time value of money. An investment yielding 100% over 10 years is significantly less impressive than an investment yielding 100% over 6 months. To normalize differing holding periods, Annualized ROI is calculated as:

$$\text{Annualized ROI} = \left[ \left( 1 + \text{ROI} \right)^{\frac{1}{n}} - 1 \right] \times 100$$

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

Can ROI be negative?

Yes. If your returned value is lower than your initial capital outlay, the ROI will be negative, representing a net financial loss on the investment.

How does ROI differ from ROAS (Return on Ad Spend)?

ROAS only measures gross revenue divided by direct advertising spend (e.g. $5 revenue / $1 ad spend = 500% ROAS). ROI factors in all operational expenses, product costs, shipping, and merchant fees to calculate true bottom-line profitability.
