How Applicant Tracking Systems (ATS) Parse Resumes

Over 95% of Fortune 500 companies and modern tech startups use automated ATS software (Workday, Greenhouse, Lever, Taleo, iCIMS) to filter job applicants before a human recruiter ever sees them.

Exact Keyword Matching

ATS parsers scan candidate resumes for specific technical keywords, job titles, and hard skills found in the original job description.

100% In-Browser Privacy

Your contact details, email address, home location, and work history remain 100% confidential and are never stored or logged on any external server.

Impact & KPI Detection

Resumes with quantifiable metrics (e.g. "Increased revenue by 28%") and active power verbs receive significantly higher interview call-back rates.

Top Rules to Pass Any ATS Resume Filter

Use Standard Section Headings

Stick to recognized titles: "Work Experience", "Education", "Skills", "Projects", "Summary". Avoid fancy names like "Where I've Been" or "My Toolbox".

Avoid Complex Multi-Column Layouts

Complex tables, graphics, text boxes, and side-by-side multi-column layouts frequently cause ATS parsers to scramble your job history out of chronological order.

Match Hard Skills to the Job Post

If the posting specifies "Python", "Docker", and "PostgreSQL", ensure those exact keywords appear verbatim in your skills and experience bullets.

Quantify Your Accomplishments

Use numbers, percentages, team sizes, and time saved to validate your impact. Example: "Managed a team of 8 engineers and boosted pipeline throughput by 34%."

Frequently Asked Questions

Is a PDF or Word (.docx) resume better for ATS?

Both PDF and DOCX are widely supported by modern ATS systems like Workday and Greenhouse. A text-selectable PDF is usually preferred because it preserves your exact visual styling and typography across all operating systems.

What is a good ATS Score?

A score of 80+ indicates strong ATS compatibility and high keyword alignment. Scores below 65 indicate missing contact details, missing standard sections, weak verbs, or low keyword overlap with the target job.
