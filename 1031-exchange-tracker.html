How Section 1031 Like-Kind Exchanges Work

Section 1031 of the Internal Revenue Code allows real estate investors to defer capital gains tax, net investment income tax (NIIT), and depreciation recapture taxes by reinvesting the proceeds from the sale of business or investment property into 'like-kind' replacement real estate.

45

45-Day Identification Period

Deliver unambiguous written identification to your Qualified Intermediary by midnight on Day 45.

180

180-Day Exchange Period

Complete settlement and acquire title to replacement property within 180 calendar days.

⚠️

No Weekend Extensions

Deadlines are strict calendar days and do NOT roll over to Monday if falling on weekends.

Frequently Asked Questions

IRC Section 1031 Timeline FAQs

What are the two critical deadlines in a Section 1031 like-kind exchange?

Under IRC § 1031(a)(3), there are two strict statutory deadlines: 1) The 45-Day Identification Period, in which the investor must formally identify replacement properties in writing by midnight of the 45th calendar day; and 2) The 180-Day Exchange Period, in which the investor must acquire and close title on the replacement properties by midnight of the 180th calendar day.

Does the 45-day or 180-day deadline get extended if it falls on a weekend or holiday?

No. Unlike other tax filing deadlines, Section 1031 deadlines are strictly calendar days. Under Treasury Regulations § 1.1031(k)-1(b)(2), there are no automatic extensions for weekends or holidays. The only exception is a federally declared disaster area via an IRS disaster notice.
